The Museum of Play is thrilled to present the [2025 Sandbox Scholars Showcase](/on-view/2025-sandbox-scholars-showcase). Each year, undergraduate college students who partake in the [Sandbox Scholars Fellowship](/learn/fellowships) are invited to present their research projects to curatorial staff and the general public. The 2025 cohort included:

- Yasmin Mansouri, Washington State University (BA in Digital Media, '28)
- Chris Adeyemi, Washington State University (BFA in Digital Media, '27)
- Sumina Shah, University of Washington (BA in Art, '27)
- Michael Lo, Seattle University (BA in Design, '27)

Over ten weeks this summer, the four fellows worked alongside our curatorial and education teams, digging into the Museum's collection and asking what play can tell us about the people who made and loved it. Their projects ranged from digital archives to hand-built prototypes, and each brought a fresh perspective to objects we thought we knew well.

## The Projects

Mansouri explored how players recorded and shared house rules for tabletop games before the internet. Drawing on handwritten rule sheets and annotated game boxes from our collection, she built an interactive timeline showing how a single game could look completely different from one kitchen table to the next.

Adeyemi researched the visual language of mid-century toy packaging. His project pairs high-resolution photographs of boxes and labels with short essays on how illustration, typography, and color shaped what children (and their parents) expected from a toy. Adeyemi also produced a set of reimagined package designs that visitors can vote on in the gallery.

Shah examined the role of handmade and folk toys in our collection, with a focus on materials and craft techniques. Her series of illustrated studies documents wooden, cloth, and paper toys from several regions, and she led a hands-on workshop where visitors tried a few of the techniques themselves.

Lo studied the design of playground equipment and public play spaces across the Pacific Northwest. Using archival photographs, site visits, and conversations with local designers, he developed a set of scale models and a short guide to what makes a play space inviting, accessible, and fun to return to.

## Join Us

The Showcase is free and open to the public. Each fellow will give a short presentation, followed by a Q&A and an informal reception in the Museum's atrium. Light refreshments will be provided, and families are welcome. Fellows' work will remain on display in the West Tower Gallery for three weeks after the event.

Please join us in congratulating this year's cohort. Their curiosity and creativity remind us that play is a serious subject worth studying.
