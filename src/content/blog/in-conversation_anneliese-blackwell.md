In celebration of her new solo exhibition, British multidisciplinary artist Anneliese Helen Blackwell sat down with the museum’s Chief Communications Officer, Phoenix Ehara, to discuss the process of creating her works.

**P.E:** There are eight works in your exhibition here at the Museum of Play, representing four different types of art. Is there one artwork that stands out to you?

**A.H.B:** Yes. Echo Scream Anguish stands out to me because, of all the pieces I created for the exhibition, this one took the longest to conceptualize and bring to life. Throughout the whole process of creating the works for The Heart Swims in Sorrow, I found it thrilling to explore new ways of expressing the key theme of the play as a vehicle for healing from adverse life events. What’s really meaningful, personally, about the exhibition is how it enabled me to develop new skills and hone existing competencies that have served me well.

**P.E:** It sounds like The Heart Swims in Sorrow is something of an artistic milestone for you.

**A.H.B:** Absolutely. The exhibition in general marks my debut as a filmmaker and choreographer. I’ve worked with literature, visual art and digital media before, which is why I composed the hypertext poems first. But directing short films and working with performing arts is definitely new territory for me. It’s been as fun as it is challenging.

**P.E:** Let’s talk about your directorial debut, Shatter My Mirror. Can you briefly describe the story?

**A.H.B:** It’s a psychological horror film centered around a 28 year-old woman who has just moved to New Orleans, away from her family in Salcombe in South West England—where I grew up, actually. She’s haunted by the memories of a traumatic event from her childhood—memories she can’t quite piece together. While she believes the move will be a fresh start, it soon becomes apparent that she’s bit off quite a lot more than she can chew.

**P.E:** You said the main character moved to New Orleans?

**A.H.B:** Yes, that’s right.

**P.E:** Is there a particular reason you chose that location?

**A.H.B:** I completed a study-abroad program in New Orleans during my undergraduate studies. I was fascinated by the culture and folklore and wanted Shatter My Mirror’s narrator to embrace that as part of her healing process.

**P.E:** What’s your favorite moment in the film?

**A.H.B:** I don’t want to spoil anything, but there’s this lovely monologue in the second act of the film where something triggers the main character to remember a traumatic experience when she was 14. She talks about sleeping under the moonlight. But, as becomes apparent later in the film, the moon represents her mother, who she’s estranged from. The relationship between mother and child is a point of trauma for her, and it’s explored in depth throughout the narrative.

**P.E:** Tell me about the process of choreographing the dance that is part of the exhibition, Echo Scream Anguish.

**A.H.B:** Sure! I worked with students from the Pacific Northwest Ballet School over the course of ten weeks to rehearse Echo Scream Anguish. The students were phenomenal and they made the daunting task of creating my first dance so exciting. The backing track I chose for the piece is really rhythmic, with a strong drum beat meant to mimic a heartbeat. I wanted the dancers’ movements to reflect that. They’re very percussive and visceral in their dancing.

**P.E:** You said you’ve previously worked with digital media and textual and visual arts before. What made you want to expand into filmmaking and choreography?

**A.H.B:** My art is centered around creating and interpreting images, whether those images are crafted from text, cinematography, or choreography. In that sense, although all these different art forms presented their own unique challenges and opportunities, they are also more alike than one may think. My previous works enabled me to connect with a lot of great people in the performing arts industry, and I used those connections to expand my practice into the disparate media of films and dance.
